Session 1

Rolling, canary and shadow releases

  • Choose a release strategy for a change and its risk
  • Send a fixed share of traffic to a new version
  • Compare two versions on errors, latency and predictions
01
Release strategies

Four ways to put a new version in front of callers

StrategyWhat callers getAnswers the question
Rollingpods replaced one at a time; all traffic moves in minutesdoes it start and stay ready?
Blue-greena full second copy; one switch moves everything, one switch backcan we undo in seconds?
Canarya small share, say 10%, then more if it holdsis it better, or at least no worse, on real traffic?
Shadownone: a copy of each request goes to it and its answer is thrown awayhow would it behave, with no caller at risk?

Day 2's rolling update answers only the first question. A model release needs the third: whether its predictions are acceptable, which no probe can tell. Shadow is the choice when even 10% of claims is too much risk, for example a model that changes who gets investigated.

Choosing

Choose the strategy from what could go wrong, and for whom

A fix to logging

No change in predictions: a rolling update, watched by the probes.

Farz 1.1.0, a new algorithm

Predictions change a little: a 10% canary with written criteria.

A new decision rule

Who gets investigated changes: shadow first, then a canary.

A new contract version

Callers must change too: a new path, /v2/, beside the old one.

The fourth case returns to Day 1: the path carries the contract version, so a breaking change to the contract never ships as a model release. Ask participants to place a release of their own team on this grid.

Lab 4.1 · the router

A hash of the claim id decides which version answers

def to_canary(claim_id: str) -> bool:
    bucket = int(hashlib.sha256(claim_id.encode()).hexdigest()[:8], 16) % 10000
    return bucket < STATE["weight"] * 10000
  • The same claim always reaches the same version: a retry never gets a second opinion
  • POST /admin/weight changes the share at once and records when and why
  • On a cluster: two Deployments behind one Service in the ratio you want, or a weighted route of a gateway or service mesh

A random split would send a retried claim to either version, so one claim could be triaged twice with different labels. Gateways such as the Kubernetes Gateway API's weighted routes, Istio or Argo Rollouts do this split in production; the course's router keeps it visible in thirty lines.

Comparing

Compare the two versions on the same hour of the same traffic

  • Errors: the share of 5xx answers of each version
  • Latency: the 95th percentile of each, measured by the caller
  • Label shares: fast track, standard, investigate
  • Agreement with the delayed labels that have arrived
What goes wrong. Comparing the canary with last week's dashboard: the traffic of a Thursday after a storm is not the traffic of a quiet Sunday.

tools/compare.py groups the replay's answers by the version that gave them. Agreement needs labels, which arrive weeks later; week 1's labels have arrived in the case study, so lab 4.1 can use them. In production that means a canary's accuracy is judged long after its errors and latency.

Checkpoint

Which release strategy?

The fraud team has a new model that changes which claims are referred to investigators. The head of the team will not let any claim be referred by it until she has seen a week of its decisions on real claims. What do you propose?

A 10% canary for a week
A rolling update with readiness probes
A shadow release for a week: copy each request to it and keep only the current model's answer
Blue-green with a switch back if anything looks wrong
Why. Only shadow traffic shows the new model's decisions on real claims while no claim is decided by it.

Answer: C. Then ask what the shadow costs: a second copy of the compute, and a store for its answers, which carries the same personal data rules as the service.

Session 2

Rollback on evidence

  • Apply written rollback criteria to a canary's metrics
  • Roll back within a time limit and record the evidence
  • Name the person accountable for the decision
02
Rollback criteria

Write the criteria before the release, while nobody is attached to it

Judge only after 200 canary requests. Roll back when any one holds:

  1. Errors (500 or 503) above 1%
  2. 95th percentile above 150 ms
  3. Any label share more than 10 points from the current version's

Munira Al-Ajmi

Wrote them, decides, and is accountable. The on-call engineer executes, and rolls back first when one fires.

From ../data/rollback_criteria.md. The third criterion is the one an ordinary web service does not have. Ask the room why the minimum of 200 requests matters: with 30 canary requests, one slow health claim moves the 95th percentile on its own.

Accountability

One named person decides; the criteria let anyone act at once

Roll back first, diagnose second. Every minute spent finding the cause is a minute of claims triaged by a faulty version.

SDAIA's AI Ethics Principles ask that responsibility for an AI system's outcomes is assigned to people; a runbook that names Munira does that for this decision. The order on the slide is the order of lab 4.2, where the check measures the seconds between the start of the release and share 0.

Diagnose second

The model file is fine; the decision rule beside it is not

"decision": {"investigate_threshold": 0.3}
"decision": {"investigate_threshold": 0.05}

Day 1 put the decision rule into the artefact so that it travels with the model. That also means a release can change behaviour without changing a single weight.

The planted fault. Ask how it could have been caught before the release: a test that compares the label shares of a new artefact with the current one on a fixed set of claims, or a review of the metadata diff. Both belong in the capstone's pipeline.

Checkpoint

What does the on-call engineer do now?

At 02:10 a canary has served 350 requests. Errors and latency are normal, but its investigate share is 14 points above the current version's. Munira's criteria say more than 10 points means roll back. Munira is asleep. What should the engineer do?

Set the canary's share to 0 now, then tell Munira with the evidence
Wait for Munira to decide in the morning
Keep the canary, since errors and latency are normal
Find the cause first, then decide
Why. The criteria were written so that the person on call can act at once; the accountable person is told, with the evidence.

Answer: A. If someone picks B, ask what the criteria are for if they do not allow action at 02:10. Accountability stays with Munira; execution does not wait for her.

Session 3

Drift in replayed traffic

  • Compute a drift statistic per feature against the training data
  • Name the shifted features and rule out a noisy one
  • Say what delayed labels allow you to conclude, and when
03
Drift

The inputs can change, or what they mean can change

Data drift

The distribution of the inputs moves: more claims through the app, larger motor amounts. Visible the day it happens.

Concept drift

The link between inputs and the right label moves: the same claim should now be triaged differently. Visible only when the labels arrive.

Rifd's labels arrive about two weeks after a claim. In week 4, weeks 1 and 2 are labelled; weeks 3 and 4 are not.

Data drift does not always hurt a model, and concept drift can happen with no data drift at all. Ask the room for an example of each in insurance: a new app channel (data), and a new fraud pattern with the same claim profile (concept).

Population stability index

PSI adds up how much each bin's share moved, weighted by how far

def psi_from_shares(expected, actual):
    e = np.clip(expected, EPS, None)
    a = np.clip(actual, EPS, None)
    return float(np.sum((a - e) * np.log(a / e)))
< 0.1
stable
0.1 – 0.25
watch
≥ 0.25
shifted

Bins are the training data's deciles for a number, its categories for a category. The clip keeps an empty bin from dividing by zero, which is lab 4.3's exercise. The thresholds are a rule of thumb from credit scoring, adopted as Rifd's policy in the case study; they are not a standard, and a team should agree its own.

Real numbers of the course's run. By week 4 the tenure p-value was 0.48: the decoy is at the edge of what 3,000 claims can detect. The amount's p-value is tiny and its pooled PSI small: the next slide shows why both are true.

Checkpoint

What should the drift report say?

In week 3, the region mix has a PSI of 0.02. Customer age has a KS p-value of 0.001 and a PSI of 0.03. The channel has a PSI of 0.31. Labels for week 3 arrive in two weeks. Which summary is right?

Age and channel shifted; the model is degrading
Channel shifted, so accuracy has fallen and the model must be retrained now
Nothing shifted: two of three features are stable
Channel shifted; age moved slightly and does not matter; accuracy is unknown until the labels arrive
Why. Name the large shift, set aside the significant but tiny move, and say what the missing labels leave open.

Answer: D. This is the shape of the drift report participants write in lab 4.3; the check also refuses a report that calls tenure shifted.

Session 4

Alerts and a runbook

  • Write an alert rule on error ratio and one on latency
  • Test both rules against a recorded error spike
  • Write a one-page runbook with the accountable person
04
Alerts

Alert on what callers feel, not on everything that can be measured

Page someone

  • errors above 1% for two minutes
  • 95th percentile above 150 ms for five minutes

Put on a dashboard

  • CPU of one pod
  • one slow request
  • a label share moving slowly: a weekly drift report
What goes wrong. An alert that fires and resolves by itself every few hours trains the on-call engineer to ignore it.

Symptom-based alerting follows the Google SRE book's advice: page on what users experience and keep causes for dashboards. The label-share criterion is for releases, where the comparison is clear; slow movement of the shares belongs to the drift report.

A rule

An expression, a duration, a severity and a runbook link

- alert: FarzLatencyP95High
  expr: histogram_quantile(0.95, sum by (le) (rate(rifd_request_duration_seconds_bucket{route="/v1/triage"}[5m]))) > 0.15
  for: 5m
  labels: {severity: page}
  annotations:
    summary: The 95th-percentile triage latency is above the 150 ms objective
    runbook_url: RUNBOOK.md#latency

Prometheus' rule format, which promlite reads too. The expression is the dashboard's query with a threshold; for: asks that it holds at every evaluation for five minutes before the alert fires. Participants write both rules without AI help first in lab 4.4.

The runbook

One page the on-call engineer can follow in the middle of the night

  • Accountable for a rollback decision: Munira Al-Ajmi
  • For each alert: what the caller sees, the first three checks
  • When to roll back, and the exact command
  • Who to tell: Munira, the platform channel, Bassam if personal data may have leaked

Linked from the alert

runbook_url: RUNBOOK.md#errors: the page opens from the notification.

The check refuses a runbook without an accountable name. Ask: who in your organisation is named in a runbook as accountable for a model's decisions? Often nobody, which is the gap this slide is about.

Checkpoint

Why does this alert flap?

An alert on the 95th-percentile latency uses a 30-second rate window and no for: duration. It fires for a minute about twice a day and resolves by itself. Callers never complain. What is the best first change?

Raise the threshold to 300 ms
Lengthen the window and add a for: duration, then test on a recording
Send it to a wider group
Delete the alert
Why. A short window and no duration react to blips; a longer window and a duration fire only on sustained breaches, which the recording can prove.

Answer: B. Then ask what the change costs: a slower alert. The recording shows how much slower.

Day 4 · wrap-up

Three things to keep from the course

Release on evidence

A canary against written criteria, compared like with like, and a log entry with its files.

Roll back first

A named person is accountable; the criteria let the person on call act at once.

Watch inputs, wait for labels

Drift by segment, significant is not large, and alerts tested before they page.

Ask each participant which of the three their team would adopt first. Retraining pipelines and online evaluation, which today only named as triggers, are the subject of AE-305. Tomorrow is the capstone: build, defend, and recover from the fault the assessor injects.
